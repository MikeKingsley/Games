# Delivery Driver road system

How the roads in Delivery Driver get from OpenStreetMap to the screen. Plans: `_docs/plans/0008-delivery-driver-real-roads.md` (real roads) and `_docs/plans/0009-delivery-driver-road-geometry-overhaul.md` (scale, junctions, layers).
Data © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL); see `scripts/bake-roads/NOTICE.md`.

Everything below is written in terms of `WORLD_SCALE` (`config.js`). It was 1:4 when plan 0009 Phase 13 wrote this and moves to 1:2 at the start of Phase 14, so figures quoted in world metres were measured at 1:4.

## Pipeline

1. **Fetch** (`scripts/bake-roads/fetch-osm.mjs`, `fetch-elevation.mjs`): Overpass JSON tiles (0.25 degree grid for main roads, coast, rivers, signals, ferries, plus one request per town for residential/service streets) and Terrarium elevation tiles (zoom 11) are cached in `.cache/` (gitignored). Re-bakes are offline.
2. **Bake** (`scripts/bake-roads/bake.mjs`, about 25 s): projects lat/lon through `world/projection.js` (plain equirectangular at `WORLD_SCALE`, no town lens; town street footprints are real-metre radii per size, `TOWN_RADIUS` is that times the scale), builds topology (shared vertices become nodes, ways split at junctions, degree-2 nodes merged, near endpoints snapped, overlaps and false crossings removed), collapses parallel one-way carriageways with the same ref into one `dual`/`M` centreline, collapses roundabouts (about 540 rings and 250 minis) to one node, types junctions (`end`, `through`, `giveway`, `signals`, `roundabout`, plus a `priority` flag), simplifies geometry (Douglas-Peucker, never creating a crossing), **generalises** for the 3D world (below), applies era rules and writes tiles. Clean-up tolerances are written in real metres and converted by the scale. Commands: `node scripts/bake-roads/bake.mjs` (offline from `.cache/`), `node scripts/bake-roads/measure-scale.mjs` (scratch bakes at other scales, writes only to `.cache/measure-scale/`), `node scripts/validate-roads.mjs --baked --area all --strict`.
3. **Era rules** (`scripts/bake-roads/era.mjs`, `history.json`): each edge gets `opened`, `closed`, `upgrades`. Precedence: curated table (M5 1977, Exeter bypass 1938, Tamar Bridge 1961, A30/A38 dualling...), OSM `start_date`, class rules (motorway opens 1977, dual carriageways start as `A` and upgrade), town growth rings (1919 core, then 1935/1955/1975/1995 with a small deterministic jitter), then propagation so nothing opens before it is reachable. The bake fails if any town centre is cut off in 1919, 1930, 1950, 1970, 1990 or 2024.
4. **Elevation** (`bake-elevation.mjs`): resampled onto a coarse grid (`elevation.bin`, format `ELV2`: heights in 1/4 world-metre steps, plane-predicted residuals as zig-zag varints with zero runs collapsed, about 1.5 MB and 0.6 MB gzipped; world metres = real metres x `WORLD_SCALE`; sea floor clamped to 0).

### Scale, no lens, generalisation

The map has no town lens (deleted in plan 0009): positions are plain equirectangular at `WORLD_SCALE`, so the 2D map is not warped around towns. Roads, vehicles and junctions are life-size while the map shrinks, so the bake generalises (`generaliseSeparation`, `consolidateJunctions`, `absorbIntoRoundabouts` in `bake.mjs`): two roads at the same level whose strips (carriageway plus 2 m pavement each side) would overlap and that do not share a node are resolved by dropping the lower-ranked road (only when connectivity, the M5/A30/A38 chain and town access survive), joining an end node onto the road beside it, contracting a short link or nudging the lower road sideways. Nearby junction nodes (2-3 within each other's footprint) are merged, and junctions inside a roundabout's ring are absorbed into it. The validator uses the same overlap model, so `overlap-same-layer` is a hard check at 0.

Vehicle speeds stay real mph, so a real mile takes `TRIP_RATE` (= `WORLD_SCALE / LEGACY_SCALE`) times longer to drive than at the old 1:13. Pay, fuel and wear per real mile are multiplied by it (`game/economy.js`, `game/contracts.js`), so income per minute of driving is unchanged. Distances shown to the player are real miles (`worldToMiles`).

### Levels and crossings

Every bridge or tunnel span in the baked data carries a `level` (bridge >= 1 from the OSM `layer`, tunnel <= -1; the stretch outside a span is level 0). Roads only share a node at the same level. `world/crossings.js` (`findCrossings`, `structureZones`) lists every place two roads cross in plan at different levels, plus each edge's `zones` (deck and tunnel intervals).

## Tile format (`world/seed/baked/`, `world/baked-format.js`)

`manifest.json` (stats, era report, curated events, `elevation` entry), `main.json` (motorway down to unclassified/lanes), `town-<id>.json` x 58 (residential, living_street, service), `coast.json` (land rings), `rivers.json` (not used at runtime), `elevation.bin`. Coordinates are quantised to 0.1 m and delta encoded; nodes have global ids (main first, then towns). Format 3: each bridge/tunnel span carries a `level` and roundabout nodes carry `ringRadius` (the OSM ring's mean radius in world metres, only a hint for the junction layout). Edges: `a, b, cls, oneway (a->b), link, lit, dual, bridge, tunnel, spans, ref, name, maxspeed (mph), town, opened, closed, upgrades, tag`. Total about 1.5 MiB gzip.

## Runtime graph (`world/roadgraph.js`)

`buildRoadGraph(region)` builds nodes and edges from the baked data, then attaches gameplay data: ferries (`addFerry`: the shortest water crossing between real street/B/A nodes near the seed's shore points), farms on real lane/B frontages outside towns, industry-site access roads at a right angle off the nearest real road, and (in `world.js`) depots, garages and in-town sites on street frontages or the edge of town. Every edge keeps its dates; all queries take the year (`edgeOpen`, `edgeClass`, `junctionType`, `edgeSpeed`). `coreNodes` finds the main strongly connected network (one-way aware); town centres and route endpoints snap into it.

Terrain (`world/terrain.js`) samples the elevation grid. `gradeRoads` cuts and fills each road to its class gradient limit (lane 12%, B 10%, A/street 8%, dual 6%, M 5%).
- **Grading with clearance:** each crossing bounds the profile of the road that has to rise (a flat top over the other road plus 3 m, ramping at its class grade) and spreads to neighbouring nodes by gradient envelopes, so a deck clears the road below by `CLEARANCE` (5.5 m) plus the `SLAB` (0.6 m). Only the deck stretch is straight; the approaches follow the land; tunnels run under their hills (`edge.underGround`). A few interchange links are relaxed (up to 2x the class limit) and some crossings that cannot be separated are taken at road level (`atGrade`, soft `crossing-at-grade`).
- **Layered `groundZ(world, x, y, hint)`:** `hint === false` is the ground (mesh building), no hint is the topmost surface (props, buildings), a number is a vehicle's own height: it stays on the surface it is on, never more than `STEP_UP` (1 m) above it, so a vehicle on a deck stays on the deck, one beneath stays beneath, and nothing falls through. `game/session.js` passes the vehicle's height; traffic takes height from its edge profile.

## Junction layout (`world/junction-layout.js`)

The single source of junction geometry (pure, no DOM/GL, lazy, cached per graph under `junctionSignature`). `layoutJunction(graph, node, year)` returns `{kind, centre, arms[], boundary[], ring, islands[], stopLines[], poles[], connectors[], reach, stats}` for kinds none, end, through, plain, mini and roundabout. Each arm has a `trim` (where its road strip starts). Kerb returns are tangent circles by class (lane 4, street/B 6, A 8, dual/M 12 m). Roundabouts are a true ring (island 4-14 m and width 6-9 m by the top road class, shrunk when the arms are short; the scaled OSM `ringRadius` is only a hint) with flared entries, splitter islands and spread arms; mini-roundabouts are a painted disc. A neighbour budget (`reachBudget`) stops two junctions' surfaces overlapping. `trimFor`, `mouthRadius` and `polygonsOverlap` are exported for the meshes, furniture, buildings and validator. Connectors (nearside lane only) are the paths traffic follows. `node scripts/junction-layout-stats.mjs` prints counts of clamped, spread, hull-fallback and rejected layouts.

## Furniture (`world/furniture.js`)

Pure rules; geometry is independent of the year (each prop has `from`/`until`, see `isLive`). It uses the junction layout of five sample years and the largest footprint. Lamps stand on lit (OSM `lit`) or town street/B/A/dual edges, staggered every 30/36/40/44 m beside the widest-ever carriageway plus kerb, never in junction mouths or on a junction surface, on bridges, in water, buildings, yards, trees or within reach of another road; rural lit roads only from 1950. Corner lamps at town junctions. Signs: country signposts, give way (from 1965) on minor arms, roundabout-ahead plates, all on the left verge of the approach. Signal poles come from the layout's stop lines. Buildings are plotted along two-way street/B/A frontages two metres beyond the widest carriageway, starting beyond the junction trims.

## Rendering (`render/gl/road-mesh.js`, `render/gl/structure-mesh.js`, Canvas fallback `render/draw-road.js`)

Per-edge ribbons (kerb strip, surface, pavements or verge, markings) start and end at the junction trims; per-node surfaces are triangulated from the layout `boundary` (kerb and pavement bands, roundabout annulus and island, splitter islands, stop and give-way lines). Era gates: centre lines 1926/1935, stop lines at signals 1935, zebra crossings 1950, double white lines on bends and give-way lines 1960, edge lines 1970. Pavements are flagstone before 1950, tarmac after.

Bridges and tunnels (`structure-mesh.js`): decks with thickness, parapets (stone before 1960, concrete after), abutments, piers every 14 m or less (a clear span over a road below) and earth embankments; tunnels are covered cuttings with side walls, roof beams, lamps from 1935 and portals. Collision with parapets and supports comes from `world/rails.js` (made on demand per year, solid only on the vehicle's own layer); the chase camera is held out of decks above it. Plan views (Canvas, minimaps, full map) draw the higher road last (`drawLevel`). The Canvas fallback draws the layout's junction polygons and roundabout islands without further detail.

## Traffic (`traffic/traffic.js`, `world/connectors.js`, `world/pathfind.js`)

One-way edges are driven forward only (spawn, `pickNext`, A*); a vehicle leaves the map at a one-way dead end. Vehicles follow the layout connectors through junctions (corner speed from the arc radius) and clockwise round roundabouts, which claim by path so circulating traffic has priority; plain and signal junctions stay single occupancy. Vehicles on different levels never block each other (`otherLayer`). `findRoute` needs a legal connector at junctions with three or more arms and prices corners by their time. Speeds come from `edgeSpeed` (class speed capped by baked `maxspeed`). Slip roads and short stubs never spawn vehicles.

## Performance rules (Slow Roads ideas, plan 0009 Phase 12)

From the web.dev Slow Roads case study, applied in the existing renderer rather than a port:
- **Corridor detail:** road meshes are built at three detail levels (`DETAIL` in `road-mesh.js`: 2 full, 1 basic, 0 coarse). Chunks within `QUALITY.<preset>.roadDetail` metres of the camera (240 / 330 / 480 m) get `roadLevel`; the rest get the coarse variant (surface and kerb on every second sample, slab-only decks, no pavements, markings or tunnels). The choice is per chunk with a 40 m hysteresis (`createFrameDriver` in `render/gl/chunk-build.js`); a swap rebuilds only the chunk's road mesh.
- **Pooling:** `createBuilder` (`meshes.js`) takes typed arrays from a free list and `finish()` copies out what was used; one builder per chunk's roads; uploads pack into one shared scratch; the frame loop and night-light gathering allocate nothing per frame.
- **Time budget and look-ahead:** `cache.budgetMs` (`QUALITY.<preset>.buildMs`: 4 / 6 / 8 ms) stops further chunk builds once a frame has spent its share; `lookAheadCoords` prebuilds chunks along the heading and the planned route (`session.route.pts`) before the frustum reaches them.
- **Cheap lookups:** `layoutJunction` answers from a stamp; it used to dominate chunk generation (74 ms down to 2 ms a town chunk).
- **Quality presets:** auto-quality steps `QUALITY.<preset>` down, which also sheds road detail. Rollback switch for measuring: `localStorage['games.dd.corridor'] = 'off'`.
- **Measure:** `node --expose-gc scripts/perf-corridor.mjs --route exeter-plymouth|a30|plymouth-truro --minutes 5 [--legacy] [--quality high] [--json]` drives the real chunk cache with no GPU and reports chunk build ms a frame, vertices and KB a chunk, GC pauses and heap. `node scripts/perf-browser.mjs --route ... --seconds 60 [--corridor off] [--gc] [--json]` runs Playwright on Edge against the dev server on port 5210 and reports rAF frame times, game CPU a frame, long tasks and heap (software GL, so compare like with like).
